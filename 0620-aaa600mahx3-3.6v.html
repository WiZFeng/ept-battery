Ⅱ. Waste Battery Management

a.Waste prevention

Use the battery correctly in accordance with the equipment manufacturer's instructions. Do not store or use the battery in extreme temperatures or high humidity. Remove the battery from equipment that will not be used for a long time to prevent leakage or unnecessary discharge. When the battery is exhausted, users are encouraged to take it to a designated collection point for potential material recovery or recycling.

b.Separate collection

End users are obligated to separate waste batteries from other household waste. Do not dispose of batteries in unsorted municipal waste or discard them carelessly. Segregated collection facilitates proper treatment and resource recovery (e.g., nickel, cobalt, rare earth elements, steel), in line with the environmental objectives of the European Union.

c.Collection points and processing

Waste batteries can be recycled through the following channels: collection bins at retail points; municipal recycling centers; and designated battery collection points. Collected batteries will undergo sorting, pretreatment (including electrolyte neutralization), and material recovery.

d.Safe operation

Avoid storing waste batteries together with metal objects to prevent short circuits. If a battery shows signs of leakage (e.g., white or crystalline residue), deformation, or overheating, wear protective gloves and store it separately in a plastic bag. Never dispose of batteries in fire, as this may cause explosions or release hazardous fumes. Keep batteries out of reach of children; if swallowed, seek immediate medical attention. This battery contains no lithium; the lithium-specific risks referred to in Article 74(1), point (d) therefore do not apply.

e.Labels and symbols

f.The impact on the environment and human health

Ni-MH batteries contain nickel compounds, rare-earth alloys (e.g., lanthanum, cerium, neodymium), cobalt and a potassium hydroxide electrolyte. If improperly handled (e.g., landfilled or incinerated), the electrolyte may leak and cause soil and water contamination; nickel compounds may accumulate in the environment. Swallowing batteries may cause injury; keep batteries out of reach of children. Improper disposal increases the environmental burden, wastes recyclable materials (e.g., nickel, cobalt, rare earth elements), and may lead to fires or injuries during waste treatment processes. Leaking batteries may cause chemical burns; handle with care. This information is provided to support end users in the responsible use and disposal of batteries, in compliance with EU waste regulations.
